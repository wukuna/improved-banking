# Exact inventory identity guards

OSRS Wiki contributors; CC BY-NC-SA 3.0. Each row in item-availability.tsv retains its pinned source URL.

The 2026-10-26 audit reconciled the full local cache with the Wiki item inventory. Exact ID and name guards distinguish interface models, animation models, unavailable copies and test-only items from their obtainable namesakes. Construction and ship-part interface identities were also checked against cache equipment slots, tradeability and inventory actions. A shared display name does not establish a shared usable item.

These records remain reviewable instead of receiving normal activity recommendations. They cannot represent a usable owned equivalent. A real item's charged, degraded or cosmetic versions are reviewed independently; this list does not make all discontinued collectibles unavailable and does not replace charge or repair rules.

The 26 October baseline retained 806 documented interface identities and 830 unavailable/event identities; later exact-ID additions are recorded below. No game state, account data or images are fetched at runtime. No saved bank groups are removed.

The same pass distinguishes cannon ammunition from handheld weapons, the quest-stage V's shield from its wearable reward, and the temporary snow imp costume from the wearable holiday reward. See the exact exclusions and pinned notes in catalogue-role-exclusions.tsv and catalogue-variant-notes.tsv.

## Historical identity follow-up — 27 October 2026

Twelve former Forestry event items had `hist`-prefixed IDs in their Wiki infoboxes. Those exact IDs now use the documented historical exclusion rather than an unexplained identity hold. They do not regain a skilling use from an old event name. Each source revision is recorded in item-availability.tsv. Other ambiguous identities remain guarded; generated symbol names alone do not establish availability.

## Exact status-table follow-up — 27 October 2026

A further 180 held IDs have explicit non-inventory evidence: 177 interface rows, one unobtainable row and two Slayer-task icon IDs. Each exact ID was checked against the copied cache name. The three custodian stalker stage aliases use the explicit juvenile/mature/elder table rows and matching guide identifiers; names alone did not supply the mapping. Two conflicting table names were left held instead of overriding the current cache.
